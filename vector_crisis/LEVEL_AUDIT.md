# Vector Crisis: Arrow Puzzle — 300-Level Audit

Audit source: `dart run tool/level_audit.dart` (per-level CSV plus the summary
below). Levels 1–3 are hand-made tutorials; levels 4–300 are generated by
`tool/generate_campaign.sh` from the rules in `tool/level_plan.dart` and then
renumbered by `tool/reindex_campaign.dart`.

## Final result

```text
Total Levels: 300
Solvable: 300
Unsolvable: 0
Exact Duplicates: 0
Mirror/Rotation Similarity Warnings (>= 0.80): 0
Difficulty ramp drops (targetMoves lower than the level before): 0
Difficulty gate misses (tool/level_plan.dart): 1
```

Every `targetMoves` equals the solver optimum, and every optimal solution is
replayed against an independent gameplay simulation in
`test/game/levels_test.dart`. The tutorial layouts are protected by the
fingerprint `1018013165`.

The one gate miss is level 231: a careful player loses it 37% of the time
against a floor of 42%. It clears the gate that matters — a player following a
fixed routine never three-stars it and loses it every time.

## Difficulty by band

The difficulty model (`lib/game/logic/level_difficulty.dart`) plays each level
240 times per player type:

- **Unplanned** taps anything that can exit, and otherwise anything that reacts.
- **Careful** plays the same way but refuses to slide a Stone onto a ray that is
  open right now — one move of lookahead.
- **Strategist** plays the routine a person works out: clear whatever can
  leave, keep Stones for last and only slide them somewhere harmless, wait for
  the Gears rather than turn a Rotator, and never waste a move. **This is the
  gate that matters.** The catalog before Gears failed it: that routine
  three-starred 52–100% of levels, which is exactly what play-testing found.

A run fails if it jams the board or overruns the move limit.

| Levels | Optimal moves | Strategist 3★ | Strategist fail | Unplanned 3★ | Unplanned jam | Careful fail |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 4–11 | 5–7 | 62% | 21% | 62% | 0% | 20% |
| 12–50 | 7–21 | 3% | 71% | 4% | 50% | 53% |
| 51–100 | 21–34 | 0% | 97% | 3% | 78% | 77% |
| 101–150 | 34–37 | 0% | 98% | 2% | 86% | 82% |
| 151–200 | 37–38 | 0% | 99% | 2% | 88% | 87% |
| 201–250 | 38–40 | 0% | 100% | 2% | 89% | 87% |
| 251–300 | 40–41 | 0% | 99% | 1% | 97% | 95% |

Levels 4–11 are the mechanic introductions and are meant to be easy; the
strategist gate only starts once Gears have been introduced. From level 51 on,
no simulated routine ever earns three stars. These are simulated players, not
play-test data.

## Pacing

- Challenge levels: every tenth level (10, 20, …, 300)
- Highest minimum solution: Level 278 — 41 moves
- Most special arrows: Level 289 — 16
- Densest board: Level 230 — 67.2% of cells
- Most exits on move one: Level 65 — 6
- Total optimal moves across the catalog: 9,629
- Move budget: `targetMoves + moveSlack`, where `moveSlack` is 2–4 depending on
  level length; three stars require the optimum

Ranges are generated in parallel, so the optimum is not monotone across range
boundaries when they come out of the generator. `tool/reindex_campaign.dart`
sorts every level from 14 on by its optimum and hands out new ids, weakest
board first within each group of equal length.

## Gameplay/solver parity

- The solver, the difficulty model and the generator share one transition
  function, `BoardEngine.move`.
- A state key holds the arrows still on the board, two bits of turn per
  Rotator, three bits per Stone recording where it landed, and two bits of Gear
  phase.
- A Stone slides to the first arrow, wall or edge and becomes a permanent wall.
  It cannot move with no free cell ahead, and bombs cannot blast it.
- Every Gear turns a quarter clockwise after every move, the one leaving aside,
  so a Gear's direction is the move count mod 4. Spending a move on nothing —
  the wait button, or a tap on something blocked — is therefore a real play and
  the solver models it as `SolverActionType.wait`.
- Frozen arrows thaw when an orthogonal neighbour leaves its cell, a sliding
  Stone included.
- A bomb removed as a victim does not chain-trigger.
- In the game, a position with landed Stones is passed to the solver through
  `LevelData.walls`. The hint follows the solver's next move, wait included. On
  Stone levels a background check ends the run as soon as the board can no
  longer be cleared.
